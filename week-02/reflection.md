# Week 2 — Reflection

## 1. What I Learned

This week, I learned the basic concepts of Machine Learning and how it is connected to AI, Deep Learning, Generative AI, and Large Language Models.

I learned about datasets, samples, features, and labels, and how they help a model learn from data. I also understood supervised learning, unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, and self-supervised learning.

I learned how a model is trained, how it makes predictions, and how we evaluate its performance using accuracy, precision, recall, and confusion matrices.

## 2. Most Important Insight

My most important insight is that machine learning is not required for every problem. We should first understand the problem, check the available data, and decide whether ML is better than a simple rule-based solution.

I also understood that a model may perform well during training but fail on new data. Therefore, data quality, proper evaluation, and understanding the risks of incorrect predictions are important.

## 3. Connection to Physical Design

As a Physical Design learner, I understood how machine learning could help predict timing problems and identify designs that need further investigation.

For example, an ML model could use historical design data, such as cell count, congestion, utilisation, and timing results, to predict whether a design is likely to meet timing.

This helped me connect machine learning concepts with my VLSI learning. However, ML predictions should support engineering decisions and cannot replace proper timing analysis or sign-off.

## 4. Challenges Faced

One challenge I faced was understanding the differences between similar concepts, such as algorithms and models, training and inference, and different learning approaches.

I also found it challenging to understand evaluation metrics and how false positives and false negatives can affect real-world results.

Working through examples related to Physical Design helped me understand these concepts better.

## 5. What I Want to Learn Next

I want to learn how to use Python to explore datasets, prepare data, train a basic machine learning model, and evaluate its predictions.

I also want to understand how ML can be applied to practical VLSI Physical Design problems and how to use AI tools responsibly in my engineering career.

## Conclusion

Week 2 helped me understand the basic concepts of Machine Learning, how models learn from data, and how their performance can be evaluated.

I now understand that choosing the right approach, using good-quality data, checking model performance, and understanding its limitations are important. I want to continue learning and explore how these concepts can be applied to real-world semiconductor engineering problems.
